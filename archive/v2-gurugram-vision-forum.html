Citizens and Governance in Partnership

Gurugram grew faster than the systems meant to run it. We are here to close that gap.

The Forum is a citizens' civic-policy platform. We help residents find the right desk for a problem, follow it through, and turn recurring problems into policy asks that the administration and elected representatives can act on.

Why the Forum exists

Two gaps keep Gurugram from working the way its residents expect.

The city has the money, the talent and the political support. What it lacks is a working link between the people who live here and the people who run it.

The governance gap

  • Expansion has outrun planning: roads, drains and waste systems were built for a smaller city.
  • Responsibility is split between MCG, GMDA, HSVP, DTCP and the district administration. Residents rarely know whose desk a problem belongs on, and agencies rarely coordinate.
  • Civic aspirations go unmet even when budgets exist, because nobody is tracking a problem from complaint to closure.

The participation gap

  • High-rise societies, working professionals and young residents have the most at stake and the least contact with the administration.
  • Goodwill for the city's leadership has not turned into structured civic participation; many residents are not even registered to vote here.
  • RWAs, corporates, universities and NGOs each work alone; their expertise never reaches the policy table together.

A city that thrives on collaboration, innovation and active participation from every stakeholder.

Vision

A world-class, community-driven and sustainable Gurugram that becomes the model for how India's fast-growing cities are run.

Mission

  • Give residents a real say in city policies and development decisions.
  • Strengthen the dialogue between civil society and the administration.
  • Channel civic energy and professional expertise into solutions that get implemented.

What we commit to do

  1. Identify and communicate urban problems to the authority that owns them, with evidence.
  2. Submit policy and planning solutions to the administration, drafted with domain experts.
  3. Build regular dialogue between residents and government bodies at the ward, sector and city level.
  4. Organise sewa and social initiatives for needs that cannot wait for a policy.
  5. Engage young residents through fellowships, internships and innovation labs.
  6. Connect political goodwill to civic delivery, so support for the city turns into visible improvement.
What we do

Six programmes, one loop: listen, solve, follow through.

Policy dialogues

Regular, minuted meetings between residents and officers from MCG, GMDA and HSVP on specific policies, with action points tracked publicly on the dashboard.

Urban innovation labs

Problem-solving workshops where experts, residents and officials design practical fixes for one problem at a time: a drain, a junction, a waste stream.

Community townhalls

Open forums, sector by sector, where residents put questions directly to the officers and councillors responsible.

Sewa drives

Volunteer action on cleanliness, greening, education and safety, run with RWAs and corporate volunteers.

Gurugram Vision Summit

An annual conclave where every stakeholder reviews the year's progress and sets the next year's priorities.

Digital platform

This site and the app that follows it: report an issue, find who owns it, track it to closure, and see what the Forum is working on.

    File with the authority too

    The Forum is not a replacement for the official complaint. Use the channel shown for your issue, note the ticket number, and add it to your report. That is what lets us escalate.

    Photos and dates win

    A dated photo, the exact spot and the earlier complaint number turn a grievance into a case an officer can act on.

    What happens next

    A Forum volunteer checks the report within three working days, maps it to the responsible desk and your ward councillor, and tells you the next step. Recurring problems across a sector go to a policy dialogue.
